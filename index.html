Our mission

Our mission is to educate K–12 youth about brain health and to advocate for policies and environments designed around the brain, guided by data-driven research.

Our vision

A brain-healthy generation, ready to navigate today’s world and shape tomorrow’s.

Our four pillars

The years between childhood and adulthood are a critical period of brain development. Yet the schools, schedules, and systems young people spend their days in aren’t always designed around what we know about the brain.

We’re here to close the gap between brain science and everyday life, and to give young people an active role in shaping the future of brain health instead of waiting for others to decide it for them.

Research

Youth-centered research into what shapes brain health, learning readiness, and brain capital, starting with district-scale research on adolescent brain health.

Brain-healthy environments

How architecture, scheduling, school design, nutrition, socioeconomic status, and other environmental factors can support healthy learning and brain development. We work with neuroarchitects, educators, and other collaborators to find out.

Brain health education

Helping students understand their own brains: what keeps them healthy, and what shapes how they learn and feel. We translate technical research into plain language anyone can use.

Youth leadership and global collaboration

Connecting young people with the researchers, physicians, educators, and organizations working on brain health, in Texas and around the world.

Our last session

Saturday, November 21, 2026Cinco Ranch Branch Library

Every session is a little different. Here’s how our last one ran.

  1. 15–20 min

    Tour the brain

    The four lobes, the cerebellum, and the brainstem, plus how neurons send messages and how practice rewires them. Expect a lot of wiggling, balancing, and remembering what you ate for breakfast.

  2. 30 min

    Be a neuroscientist

    Sculpt a brain out of clay, build neurons from pipe cleaners, and play brain-trick games and mini Jeopardy for prizes.

  3. 10 min

    Keep it healthy

    Sleep, food, movement, friends, and breaks: the everyday habits that get a brain ready to learn.

Our founding year, 2026–27

Four milestones, connected like a pathway: each one sets off the next.

  1. Build the founding student council

    Recruit students across Texas who are interested in neuroscience, education, research, architecture, policy, communications, and more.

  2. Establish an adult advisory network

    Bring together advisors from science, medicine, education, architecture, and organizational development.

  3. Launch our first programs

    Develop the council’s first research, brain-health education, and brain-healthy-school initiatives.

  4. Grow toward statewide collaboration

    Connect students and schools across Texas, then build partnerships beyond the state.

What guides us

Inclusion

Brain health is for everyone, so every perspective belongs at the table, starting young.

Empathy

Behind every data point is a person. We listen before we advocate.

Patience

Brains develop over years, and so does real change. We’re building for the long run.
