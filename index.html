Our mission

Our mission is to educate K–12 youth on brain health and to advocate for policies and environments designed around the brain, guided by data-driven research.

Adolescence is when the brain does its biggest remodel.

The years between childhood and adulthood are a critical period of brain development. Yet the schools, schedules, and systems young people spend their days in aren’t always designed around what we know about the brain.

We want to close the gap between brain science and everyday life, and give young people an active role in shaping the future of brain health instead of waiting for it to be decided for them.

Four ways we put neuroscience to work

Research

Youth-centered research into what affects brain health, learning readiness, and brain capital, starting with district-scale adolescent brain-health research in Katy ISD.

Brain-healthy environments

How architecture, light, sound, schedules, and school design can support healthy learning. We work with neuroarchitecture and healthy-school collaborators to find out.

Brain health education

Helping students understand their own brains, what keeps them healthy, and what shapes how they learn and feel. We translate technical research into plain language.

Youth leadership and collaboration

Connecting young people with the researchers, physicians, educators, and organizations working on brain health in Texas and around the world.

Meet Your Brain!

Our hands-on workshop for kids ages 8–13 and their families, led by local high school students. It’s free, hands-on, and nobody leaves without building a neuron.

Who
Kids 8–13 and their parents
Where
Local libraries and schools around Katy
Cost
Free
  1. 15–20 min

    Tour the brain

    The four lobes, the cerebellum, and the brainstem, plus how neurons send messages and how practice rewires them. Expect a lot of wiggling, balancing, and remembering what you ate for breakfast.

  2. 30 min

    Be a neuroscientist

    Sculpt a brain out of clay, build neurons from pipe cleaners, and play brain-trick games and mini Jeopardy for prizes.

  3. 10 min

    Keep it healthy

    Sleep, food, movement, friends, and breaks: the everyday habits that get a brain ready to learn.

  2. Establish an adult advisory network

    Bring together scientific, medical, educational, architectural, and organizational advisors.

  3. Launch our first programs

    Develop the council’s first research, brain-health education, and brain-healthy-school initiatives.

  4. Grow toward statewide collaboration

    Connect students and schools across Texas, then build partnerships beyond it.

What guides us

Inclusion

Brain health is for everyone, so every perspective belongs at the table, starting young.

Empathy

Behind every data point is a student. We listen before we advocate.

Patience

Brains develop over years, and so does real change. We’re building for the long run.
